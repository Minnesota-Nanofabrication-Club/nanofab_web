{{/* ==============================================================
     robots.txt — the first file a search engine asks for.

     Turned on by `enableRobotsTXT = true` in hugo.toml. Hugo renders
     this template to public/robots.txt on every build.

     WHAT IT SAYS: crawl everything, and here is the sitemap. That is
     the right answer for a public club site — there is nothing here
     to hide, and a missing robots.txt is not "allow all", it is just
     a 404 the crawler has to guess around.

     WHY THE SITEMAP URL IS BUILT, NOT TYPED: the Sitemap: directive
     is the one line in robots.txt that MUST be a full absolute URL
     with the scheme and host — a relative "/sitemap.xml" is invalid
     and silently ignored. absURL hangs it off baseURL in hugo.toml,
     so it keeps the /nanofab_web/ subpath and follows the site if it
     ever moves to its own domain. Never hand-type it: typing
     "/sitemap.xml" is exactly how you end up pointing at the
     organisation's root instead of this project's.

     ONE CATCH, worth knowing before you trust this file:
     crawlers only ever read robots.txt from the ROOT of a host, i.e.
     minnesota-nanofabrication-club.github.io/robots.txt. This site
     lives in a subdirectory, so what we publish lands at
     .../nanofab_web/robots.txt and Google will not look for it there.
     It is still correct to ship — it costs nothing, it is right the
     moment the club gets its own domain, and tools that are pointed
     at it directly will read it. Until then, submit the sitemap URL
     below to Google Search Console by hand (README 4.19); that is
     what actually gets the pages crawled. A root robots.txt would
     have to be served from a separate repo publishing to the
     organisation's github.io root, which is not ours to touch.
     ============================================================== */ -}}
